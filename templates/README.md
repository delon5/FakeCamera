# Ready-made templates

This directory is a ready-to-copy `ux0:data/FakeCamera/`: a `config.txt` that knows the titles using the camera, and pictures drawn for what each of them expects to see. Copy everything into `ux0:data/FakeCamera/` on the memory card and FakeCamera picks the right picture from the title ID.

Every picture is generated by `tools/make_templates.py` (no copyrighted artwork). To use your own photos instead, convert them with `tools/make_template.py` (see the end of this page).


## The pictures

| File | Size | Content | Made for |
|---|---|---|---|
| `face.bmp` | 640x480 | A drawn frontal face, with the light and dark areas face detectors look for | Front camera by default; face detection and smile detection |
| `pattern.bmp` | 640x480 | Colour bars, a checker board, a gradient and the FakeCamera name | Back camera by default; photos used as stickers, textures, puzzles or decals |
| `objects.bmp` | 640x480 | Six big objects of different colours on a table | "Show me something red" type of mini-games |
| `room.bmp` | 960x540 | A living room panorama, wider than the camera: tilt to look around | AR games playing in "your surroundings" |
| `arena.bmp` | 640x480 | An outdoor backdrop (sky, hills, lawn) | AR games putting characters into the picture, fight backgrounds |
| `ar-table.bmp` | 640x480 | The six PlayStation AR Play cards on a table (1-2-3 in a row, 4 and 5 beside them, 6 above) | AR Play titles needing several cards |
| `ar-card.bmp` | 640x480 | One big AR Play card (card 1) | AR Play titles needing a single card |
| `qr.bmp` | 320x240 | A QR code opening the FakeCamera GitHub page | QR code scanners (VitaShell) |
| `avatar.bmp` | 64x64 | A tiny camera icon | Titles which only accept small pictures (WipEout 2048) |
| `white.bmp` | 320x240 | Plain white | "Bright light" checks |
| `barcode-1.bmp` to `barcode-4.bmp` | 640x480 | Four valid EAN-13 barcodes (4901234567894, 4902102072618, 5012345678900, 8712345678906) | Barcode scanners: listed together, the plugin shows them in turn |

The AR Play markers are the real 5x5 codes of the six official cards (redrawn, without the PlayStation logo); the AR tracking only reads the black and white code, so the games should recognise them like the printed cards. What happens next differs per game: a still picture is a still card, so the game's objects appear and stay put, like a camera on a tripod. Tilt scrolling on a picture wider than the camera (`room.bmp`) is the only "movement" available.

## Your own pictures

`tools/make_template.py` converts any JPG or PNG into a BMP that FakeCamera accepts, at the right size:

```sh
# a photo of you, as the front camera picture of every title
python3 tools/make_template.py me.jpg --name face.bmp
# a scan of your inviZimals trap card, referenced from the title's section
python3 tools/make_template.py trap.png --name trap.bmp --fit contain --background white
# a wide picture of your room, to look around by tilting
python3 tools/make_template.py room.jpg --name room.bmp --size 1280x720
# named after a title, the old way, for the back camera only
python3 tools/make_template.py card.png --title PCSF00062 --camera back
```

Copy the result into `ux0:data/FakeCamera/` and, for free names, reference them with `image=`, `front=` or `back=` in `config.txt`. Several names separated by commas take turns: at each camera opening, or every `cycle` seconds while the camera stays open. The script needs Python 3 and Pillow (`pip install pillow`).
